The potted plants: round A and round B

Each row is one view of the same plant, left to right: before (the first drawing, or for daisy, tulip, rose and peony the emoji sketch) and v1. Three views per plant: the stages by day and dusk with the four moods, the grown plant at card size by day, dusk and night with the thirsty and dozing moods, and close crops of the bloom and the leaves. Numbers are in RULES.md, section "Plants".

Sunflower

A Fibonacci spiral of 150 seeds with a ring of tiny florets, 21 ray petals each a little different (length, width, curl), green bracts behind, a furry stem, big veined heart leaves; a bee when the house is thriving, a fallen petal on the soil.

Basil

Fewer, fuller leaves: cupped, glossy ovals with veins and a glint; the tuft of young leaves gives way to a tall white flower spike, whorls of two-lipped florets in green cups.

Monstera

Fenestrated heart leaves that cut themselves as they age (whole heart, slits, then slits and holes), a moss pole tied with twine, aerial roots out of the nodes. The richness the owner loved is kept.

Cactus

A ribbed cylinder lit across its ribs, valleys and crests, fine fans of spines on an areole at every crest, a waxy flower with a glint on each petal.

Daisy

New: a rosette of spoon leaves and three flower stems that open one after another; long white rays that blush at the tip around a golden disc of florets.

Tulip

New: broad strap leaves in a clasping fan, three stems (open, half open, bud); a waxy cup of six tepals flamed with yellow at the base that nods when thirsty and shuts when dozing.

Rose

New: arching canes with thorns, compound leaves of five serrated leaflets (bronze while new), three blooms: a rose, a cup and a bud; satin petals with a rolled light edge and a wound heart.

Peony

New: deeply cut glossy leaves, three stems carrying a big ruffled bloom in four rings, a half-open one and a round green bud with a pink slit; a crown of gold stamens.

Round B

The eleven plants that were still emoji sketches, and the rose's bloom. Each plant: before (the emoji sketch, or for the rose the first v1 bloom), then v1 (stages by day and dusk with the four moods; the card at day, dusk, night, thirsty, dozing; close crops). Numbers are in RULES.md, section 15. All twelve at the shop's size.

Mint

A bush with the leaf, stem and runners of its own: puckered, toothed leaves with six pairs of veins, a plum-tinted stem, a third branch, and two runners that arch over the pot's rim with a pair of small leaves at every node; a short lilac spike.

Rosemary

Seven woody stems dressed in needles (a thin triangle each, in three greens by the light, with a silver line on the lit ones), pairs alternating between spread and foreshortened; small two-lipped pale blue flowers in the axils and a cluster of buds at each tip.

Snake plant

Nine sword blades, each one outline from a sampled spine, widest low down and tapering to a point: pale chevron bands across, a dark rib, a yellow margin, a lit half. New blades are spears that unfurl; two pups at the foot. It never blooms.

Fern

Nine pinnate fronds in a vase: the centre ones upright, the outer ones bowing over the rim, each with pairs of pinnae that are longest a third of the way up. The young ones are bronze fiddleheads that unroll as they grow; spore dots on the mature outer fronds.

Lavender

A mound of grass-thin grey-green leaves and seven thin stems that end in spikes of whorled florets (a hood and a facing floret to a whorl, grey-green calyx under each, deep violet at the base and lighter at the tip), opening from the bottom up.

Orchid

A moth orchid: four thick broad leaves, silver-green aerial roots with green tips over the rim, and one spike that arches over carrying seven blooms (sepals, two wing petals, a magenta three-lobed lip, a column) that open from the foot and end in buds.

Bonsai

A thick, twisting trunk that flares into roots, with bark fissures and a lit ridge, a pale stub of deadwood (a jin), moss at the foot, three branches and a crown, each ending in a flat cloud of small round leaves in four tones.

Maple

A slender trunk that forks into layered branches, each with a cloud of red and orange palmate leaves; leaves on the soil. The leaf drops to olive when thirsty.

Pumpkin

A vine that creeps over the pot's rim with big lobed leaves on long stalks, tendrils that curl, a yellow trumpet flower and a ribbed fruit built from meridians (each lobe toned by the sun), green when small and orange when ripe. Leaves hang limp when thirsty.

Pine

A straight trunk with eight whorls of drooping needle sprays that make a cone, shorter at the top, a leader of new growth, and two cones hanging among the needles.

Snowdrop

Glaucous strap leaves and three stems that hook over so that a bell hangs from each: a green ovary, three long white outer tepals that flare like a skirt, a short inner cup with a green mark at the mouth.

Rose, the bloom

The round A bloom read as a flat pinwheel of petals, like a camellia. Now: outer petals that reflex with a broad rolled lip catching the light and a line of shade where it meets the petal, a bowl of upright petals, a dark cup, and tight petals wound round the heart, each a disc whose lit edge shows as a crescent where the next begins.
