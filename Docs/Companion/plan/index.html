Nestie · companion plan · version 10 · 24 October 2026

A companion that grows with the house

Your household picks a plant or a pet. It lives at the top of Today and grows from what the house already does: chores ticked, the shopping bought, dinner decided, a leak fixed, a movie night that happened. In about two weeks it's fully grown: a short ceremony, a card in the album, Leaves for the shop, and the next one chosen together. Everything ships in one release.

Segment switches that don't hitch

  • Screens are kept. Each segment is built the first time it's opened and kept after that, with its scroll position and live data. Only the shown one puts buttons and search in the bar, and the entrances still replay on every switch.
  • A switch is split over two moments. The fade and the underline land on the tap; the bar, search and entrances follow 50 ms later, so no single frame carries all of it.
  • Big screens build in steps. Calendar, Money and Problems draw their bar first and the rest a frame at a time, pixel-for-pixel the same picture.
  • On your phone (Release): first switch 42–86 ms, going back 31–45, again 25–50. The simulator said 77–292, and most of that turned out to be the simulator itself.

What happens with animals?

The same seven stages: a duckling hatches from an egg, a kitten arrives in a little basket. Each stage it gets bigger and learns something new (first wobbly steps, playing, a trick). It reacts when someone does something, naps when the house is quiet, and steps into the album when grown.

What is a "Rive rig"?

Rive is an animation tool with a player that runs inside apps. A rig is the character's skeleton and controls, like strings on a puppet, so the app can make it blink, hop or sleep live. One four-legged rig can wear a kitten's or a puppy's drawing.

Who can make it →

Can an illustrator or design team handle it?

Partly. Any good illustrator can draw the plants, pets and decorations. Rigging and animating in Rive is a different skill: you need a 2D animator who uses Rive, or a studio with both. A one-week paid trial tells you for sure, and there's a fallback that needs only an illustrator.

The shop, on the app's own design

v8's shop was a solid plum room with drawn coins and lipped buttons, and you said it looked like it came out of a game. Now it's an ordinary NestZone screen that happens to sell things, and the pressed-in feel you liked stays on the one button that spends. These are from the simulator at night, so the windows are at dusk; by day they're lit.

How it's built

  • Glass cards on the app's backdrop, the theme's accent and the app's type. Money is leaf.fill and sparkle, coloured on the glyph only (Amount).
  • The only button that spends Leaves, Sparkles or real money is pressed in (.depth); everything else is the app's usual buttons.
  • The window each thing stands in is the companion's sky at that hour (ShopStage).
  • The purchase card runs on past the home indicator: no strip of screen under it.
  • Shelves are pages, so switching never flashes empty or lands past a short shelf's end.

Motion, everywhere

  • One tap, one reaction: Water's drops stack like the sparkles do, instead of restarting.
  • Coins fly from the jar to the thing; a pack's sparkles land in the pinned counter, every time.
  • Segment switches replay their entrances, and the underline stretches as it slides.
  • Money and charts settle without bouncing; the budget ring counts up with its sweep.
  • Settling up: the balance shrinks away, a seal stamps in and draws its check, the haptics build to a success.
  • Every tap is felt once, and every motion has a Reduce Motion version. The rules are in CLAUDE.md.

What the research says

What works in SumOne and the others, and what hurts

SumOne's EggMon grows when both partners answer the daily question; people love the shared ritual, and lose interest once it's fully grown with nothing after it. Fifteen products were studied: what they reward, what they punish, how they end, what they sell, and what reviews say.

  • Grow it on the household's totalNever one person's bar. Pikmin Bloom's Party Walk adds everyone's steps and ranks nobody. Chore-app research: people get defensive when told they're not equal partners.
  • Nobody can kill itIt can wilt, and one action from anyone wakes it. Plant Nanny² replaced death with wilting after complaints; Finch's bird never dies and reviewers single that out.
  • One person's lapse never hurts everyoneForest's "if anyone gives up, the whole forest falls" and Habitica's party damage point at a culprit. In a home with an uneven load that's poison.
  • Nobody waits on anybodySumOne locks you out until your partner answers; Widgetable's fix was "no more taking turns".
  • Count every kind of contributionPlanning dinner, restocking and reporting a leak are work too. Effort-weighted points moved one couple from 90–10 to 60–40.
  • Give it a finite life you can seeSumOne's motivation drops once the pet is grown ("takes forever to evolve", then nothing). Show the next stage and a rough "days to go".
  • Graduation starts the next one; the album keeps the storyTamagotchi hands over to a new generation; Neko Atsume's album and Forest's timeline are what people come back for.
  • Pausing costs nothingApple added ring pauses after "even my rest days didn't feel restful". A holiday mode for the whole house.
  • Notifications celebrate, never guiltPengu users got "four alerts saying the penguin is dying". Milestones go in the feed; at most one gentle nudge.
  • Sell decoration, never careSumOne's shop sells room décor and its top complaint is too few free pebbles; Widgetable's ads-for-food made it "a chore". Earn enough to feel generous, buy to go further.

Care, the daily ritual

  • Tap it: today's need shows up (water, mist, turn it to the light; for a pet: pet, feed, play). A soft haptic, leaves lift for a second.
  • Each person counts once a day; two or more on the same day earn a "together" bonus and a small moment for the first one next time they open the app.
  • Care is 12–31% of growth, so it matters but isn't a clicker: care alone would take 38 days.

Fair without keeping score

  • Growth is the household's total. No per-person bar, no ranking, and the companion never mentions the chore split.
  • A partner who never cares costs the couple two days, not the plant's life.
  • The recap names people only if everyone took part, and then only with a warm line ("Ece watered it most mornings"), never a count.

The design · after graduation

The album keeps every one the house has grown

Each graduate becomes a card: its portrait (redrawn from its seed, so nothing is stored but numbers), the dates, the days it took and three moments from its life. Graduation pays 100 Leaves into the household's wallet and leaves a keepsake for the scene.

Choosing the next one

  • Only one person to ask? No vote. A household of one, or a household where only one person is on the new version, gets the picker straight away.
  • Two or more: "Who comes next?" offers three candidates (a plant, a pet, the newest arrival), swiped like movie night. It closes when everyone has voted or after 24 hours.
  • Most yeses wins; a tie goes to something the house hasn't grown yet; if nobody votes, the first person to open the picker chooses.
  • A new seed starts growing the moment the last one graduates, so nothing done while the house decides is lost; the species is applied when the vote closes.
  • Candidates come from what the household owns: free and earned species, plus anything someone bought for the house.
  • Built on the app's existing polls as a generic poll. A new poll type would show up as a movie round on every older version.

Why spots, not anywhere

  • The card is small. Free placement on a phone-width card turns into clutter that covers the plant within a week.
  • Every phone draws the same scene. A spot is a place in the scene, not a pixel, so the pumpkin sits in the same place on an SE and a Pro Max, and on everyone's phone in the house.
  • It's accessible. Each spot is a button VoiceOver can read: "Floor, left: Jack-o'-lantern. Double-tap to change."
  • The server can check it. Eight small fields: is this item the house's, and does it fit this spot?
  • It still feels like yours: any spot item can be flipped, and the tree takes four things at once.

How it's built

  • App: the spots are SwiftUI overlays on the scene at fixed fractions of its size; draggable and dropDestination for drag, taps for everything else; a haptic on snap.
  • Server: one companion_scene row per home, and companion:place writes one spot at a time, so two people decorating different spots never overwrite each other.
  • Checks: the item is the house's own (or free), it fits the spot, the tree has room. A purchase that leaves with its buyer empties its spot.
  • Items are data: a picture plus effects the app already knows (glow at night, flicker, sway, twinkle, fall), so new seasonal things arrive without an app update.

How the dates are worked out

  • Computed, not listed, wherever possible: fixed days, "the second Sunday of May", offsets from Western or Orthodox Easter, and days in the Islamic (Umm al-Qura), Hebrew, Chinese and Persian calendars that iOS already knows.
  • Verified tables for the almanac days iOS doesn't carry: India's official holiday list for 2026–27 and Drik Panchang (New Delhi) for 2028–30. A year missing from a table has no date, never a guessed one.
  • Regional rules: Mothering Sunday in Britain, 10 May in Mexico, 21 March across the Arab world; Father's Day on St Joseph's in Italy and Spain; Singapore's Vesak from the Chinese calendar.
  • Checked: every computed day for 2026–27 matched official sources, including Diyanet's Ramazan Bayramı (9 March 2027) and Kurban Bayramı (16 May 2027). Details in Docs/Companion/HOLIDAYS.md.

Rules for every holiday

  • Each house chooses its days in "Our holidays"; the suggestions come from its country and can all be changed.
  • No snow on a December tree south of the equator.
  • Religious days only ever get free things, never things to buy. Solemn days (Yom Kippur, Ashura, Good Friday alone) aren't dressed up at all.
  • For the shop later: one free thing per event earned together, a few to buy from the jar, all back every year, a plain end date and no ticking clock.
  • An Islamic date can be a day apart from a country's own announcement; the app says "about" for those.

The catalog

Wide enough for a house that grows one every two weeks

An active household graduates about 26 companions a year, so the catalog starts at 39 and grows by two or three every month, with seasonal species that return each year. Every species also has three colour variants to grow again later. Some come free, some are earned with Leaves, some are bought.

Trees, the duckling and the decorations are drawn in code already; the other icons are stand-ins until they are.
The house jar · Kumbara

One jar, two currencies, one price per thing

Every house has a jar, like the one on the kitchen shelf. It holds Leaves, which only progress gives — growing, new stages, graduations — and are never sold, and Sparkles, which are what people buy, with a few earned along the way like a merge game's level-up. Everybody in the house spends from it. Every thing in the shop has one price in one currency: never "100 Leaves or 5 Sparkles" for the same thing, because a second way to pay for what progress already earns is paying to skip the progress. A house short of Leaves can make up the rest with its own Sparkles, one for one: the same price, paid partly in the other coin, never a cheaper one. Sparkles are never made up with Leaves. Nothing is bought without a last look at what it costs and what's left; not enough even so, and the question offers the Sparkles store, then comes back to the thing once there's enough.

  1. Ece and Mert choose their first companion. A welcome gift lands in the jar with a little celebration: 🍃 150 and ✨ 180.
  2. They put up bunting (🍃 100) and a paper lantern (✨ 120). Still 🍃 50 and ✨ 60 in the jar.
  3. Every stage pays 🍃 10 and ✨ 5; growing Manolya all the way pays 🍃 100 and ✨ 25.
  4. Mert wants the Forest friends pets (✨ 900). He adds Sparkles to the jar with real money; the feed says "Mert added 1,000 Sparkles to the jar".

🍃 Leaves · progress, never sold

  • 150 as the welcome gift; 10 at each new stage; 100 at every graduation.
  • Buy the everyday things: bunting, pots, fairy lights, a kilim, the earned species.
  • Can't be bought, ever — so a house that never pays always has something to save for.

✨ Sparkles · purchases, plus a few earned

  • 180 as the welcome gift; 5 at each new stage; 25 at every graduation — enough to try a premium thing now and then without paying.
  • Bought in packs by anyone in the house, into the house's jar. They never expire, as Apple requires.
  • Buy the premium things: lanterns, scenes, golden pots, premium species and pet packs.

Keeping a shared jar friendly

  • Anyone in the house can spend; the feed says who added and who spent, so the jar is never a mystery.
  • Later, if wanted: "ask before spending over 300".
  • Children's top-ups go through Ask to Buy.
  • Prices in money are shown next to Leaves when topping up.
  • If Apple refunds a Sparkles purchase, the jar's Sparkles go down by that much and can dip below zero; the premium shelf pauses until it's back. Nothing already bought is taken away.

Why this works with Apple

  • Sparkles are consumable purchases, which can be bought again and again.
  • Apple refunds only to the person who paid, and Sparkles can't be cashed out or moved between houses, which is what Apple asks of gifts.
  • No random boxes: Apple would require published odds, and they're a trust problem in a family app.

When the catalog runs out

"You've grown them all"

A busy house can grow everything available before the next monthly drop arrives. That moment deserves its own celebration, not an empty picker. When the server has nothing new, the house gets a finale and a way to keep going.

What happens then

  • The finale: the whole album pans past, the totals for everything the house has grown, a crown keepsake, and 500 Leaves for the jar.
  • Grow a favourite again: any species can be grown a second and third time in a new colour (moonlight, golden), so collecting has depth beyond the list.
  • A wildflower meadow keeps growing quietly in the meantime, with no graduation, so Today is never empty.
  • New seeds arrive: the date of the next drop, and a one-time "they're here" notification when the server adds new species.
  • What "all" means: everything anyone can get without paying: the free species, the ones bought with Leaves, rewards, and the seasonal ones currently in season. Paid items are never counted, so buying a pack can't push the crown further away.
  • Specials shelf: paid species and packs have their own row in the album, with a small "all grown" moment when every pet in a pack has been raised.
With your subscription plans

The companion stays free, and grows just as fast without a subscription

You're planning to put things like House problems, Money, the calendar, custom movie collections and custom recipes behind a subscription. The companion should stay free: it's the reason to open the app every day, and the best way to show people what a subscription adds.

The same pace for everyone

  • Fixing problems and events that happen are growth sources. Without them, a free couple would take 16 days instead of 14 (simulated).
  • So when a house doesn't have those features, chores, dinners and movie nights count a little more. That brings it back to 14 days, the same as a subscribed house.
  • That keeps the promise that money never buys growth, even indirectly.

What a subscriber could get here

  • A monthly 300 Leaves into the house jar.
  • One exclusive seasonal species or scene each month.
  • Never faster growth or an easier finale.
  • Worth deciding together with the rest of the subscription, including whether one person's subscription covers the whole house.

Putting existing features behind a subscription deserves its own plan. One server serves every app version, and older versions don't know about subscriptions, so the locking has to be done carefully to avoid breaking screens people already use.

Art and motion · all in code

No illustrator, no Rive, no bill: everything is drawn in code

You asked me to be the illustrator, the animator and the motion designer, and to make the trees premium. Two directions were drawn in code and put side by side; you chose layered gouache: more detailed, more realistic, more painterly. Nothing is a picture. The trees grow from the household's id, the duckling is a handful of shapes, every decoration is a few dozen lines of drawing, and every movement is a formula. It costs nothing to license, adds nothing to the download, and can change in a day.

How a gouache tree is built

Leaf-shaped lobes are laid in clumps, like cut paper, on three depth tiers: cool and dark at the back, warm and lit in front, with the light always from the upper left. Each clump has a shadow shape, a lit copy with a sun gradient, a sunny patch and a few leaf cut-outs. The front clumps cast shadows on the ones behind and on the branches, a rim light picks out the back at night, and every flower has its own gradient and stamens.

  • Every house's tree is its own, grown from the household's id. The app's tree matches its prototype number for number.
  • Moods are a smooth scale, not four pictures. Bright lifts and warms it; thirsty turns it olive and hangs its clumps; dozing goes pale straw but stays one tree.
  • Far less to make. 25 plants × 7 stages × 3 colours × 4 moods would be 2,100 paintings; this is a few hundred lines per species.

Both directions side by side: compare.html. The gouache tree live, with sliders: tree.html.

Smooth, and how it stays that way

  • The tree is drawn only when it changes: a mood easing in, a stage growing, the light moving with the clock. Between those, the same picture is reused.
  • The wind bends that picture about the root, so the pot never moves and nothing is redrawn.
  • It holds still while Today scrolls and when a sheet covers it; at half height, like the Lab, it keeps playing.
  • Measured, not guessed: Today holds 60 frames a second with a grown, decorated tree, at rest and while scrolling fast.
  • Tried and dropped: Metal shaders for the wind and a painted grain. Every frame waited for them and Today fell to about 25 frames a second. They're kept, with notes, in art/shaders.

No paid tools

  • Everything is SwiftUI Canvas and TimelineView, which ship with iOS. No custom shaders.
  • Rive's player is free, but making animations needs its editor and an animator; we need neither.
  • What code still can't do: real brush texture, and rich character acting (a cat curling up to sleep). Pets stay simple and expressive: eyes, posture, colour and a few moves each.
  • The prototypes in plan/ and art/ are the same drawing in a browser, for trying ideas before they go into the app. The live demo above still uses the earlier tree; the gouache one is tree.html.
Server and app

Counted on the server, so every version feeds it

The actions that grow it are ones every installed version already sends. Each gets a one-line hook that schedules a small "reconcile" job after the write, so the companion can never slow down or roll back a tick. Purchases are checked on the server too: the phone hands over Apple's signed receipt, the server verifies it and credits the house jar once.

New functions

  • companion:forHome: one live payload with stage, progress, mood clock, today's care, the last 8 growth events to animate, any ceremony or finale waiting.
  • companion:care, companion:choose (direct pick for one), companion:markCeremonySeen, companion:album.

  • Internal: reconcile, an hourly sweep (which also puts seasonal things away at an event's end and back on its first day), recount, and a monthly "new seeds" notice.

Safe for older versions

  • Only additions: new tables and functions, hooks inside existing writes, no argument changes.
  • Older phones keep growing it by ticking chores; they just don't see it. Pushes go only to people on the new version.
  • A kill switch on the server turns crediting off without a release; the shop has its own.

The first day

  • No companion until someone on the new version opens Today; old-version homes are left alone.
  • No backfill of years of chores. A small head start from the last week's chores, so the pot isn't bare.
  • Untick a chore and its growth goes back, like the chore split; tidying the shopping list never shrinks it.
