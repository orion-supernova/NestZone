Free and Pro

The owner's tiering, made exact. The rule underneath it: Pro gates features, never the household's own data. A house that stops paying keeps reading everything it wrote; it just can't add more in the Pro places.

One rule for every house without Pro, whether it never subscribed or stopped renewing: what is already there stays readable, new things need Pro. Calendar and Money open read-only with a banner and an "Upgrade" button when the house has data in them, and on a locked preview when it has none; their reminders stop. Custom movie lists and categories stay visible and their items can be removed. Nothing is ever deleted because a card expired.

Who pays: the household

Apple sells a subscription to one Apple ID. NestZone's data belongs to a house. If Pro were per person, two people would open the same party and one would see a budget the other can't, which is worse than not having the feature. So any member with an active Pro subscription makes the whole house Pro. One subscription covers a house of five; that is the selling point, and it's why the other four care whether it renews.

  • No owner field needed. A house is Pro while some current member holds an unexpired entitlement. If the payer leaves, Pro leaves with them, with no stale plan_source to clean up.
  • A person in two houses lifts both. That's acceptable and simple; revisit only if it's abused.
  • The paywall says who is carrying it: "Murat's Pro covers this house."
  • Family Sharing: on. A family that shares purchases is almost always the same household.

Architecture

RevenueCat runs the purchase, the receipts and the renewals. Convex stays the only judge of what a house may do, because a client-side check is decoration: every old build and every modified client talks to the same server.

flowchart LR
  A[iPhone · RevenueCat SDK] -- purchase / restore --> B[App Store]
  B -- receipts --> C[RevenueCat]
  A -- "pro:refresh (after purchase, on launch)" --> D[Convex action]
  D -- "GET /v1/subscribers/{userId} (secret key)" --> C
  D --> E[(pro_entitlements)]
  F[cron: every 6h, near-expiry rows] --> D
  C -. "webhook (later, needs tunnel rule)" .-> G[POST /revenuecat]
  G --> E
  E --> H["requirePro(home) · homePro(home)"]
  H --> I[Pro mutations refuse]
  H --> J[notifyHome drops finance / calendar pushes]
  H --> K["pro:status (live query) → app gates"]

Why pull before push

HTTP actions live on the site origin (:3211) and the tunnel forwards only /.well-known/* there today. A RevenueCat webhook therefore needs a new ingress rule before it can work. Pulling instead needs nothing new: after a purchase or a restore, and once per launch, the app asks the server to check; the server asks RevenueCat with the secret key and writes the answer. A cron re-checks rows that are about to expire so a cancelled subscription ends on time even if nobody opens the app. The webhook is added later as a faster path, not a dependency.

Server pieces

PieceWhat it is
pro_entitlementsOne row per user: user_id, expires_at (null = lifetime), product_id, store, is_sandbox, will_renew, checked_at. Index by_user, by_expires.
lib/pro.tshomeProStatus(ctx, home) reads each member's row (a house is at most a handful of people) and returns { pro, carriedBy, expiresAt }. requirePro(ctx, homeId) throws PRO_REQUIRED. Respects PRO_ENFORCE.
pro:statusLive query by home, so a purchase on one phone unlocks the others in the house within a second.
pro:refreshAction. Calls RevenueCat with REVENUECAT_SECRET_KEY, maps the pro entitlement into the row. Sandbox purchases count only while PRO_SANDBOX=on (TestFlight and review), the same pattern as JAR_ENVIRONMENTS.
pro:sweepCron every 6 h: refresh rows expiring within 24 h or expired within the last 3 days (billing grace).
push.notifyHomeOne check: a finance or calendar category for a non-Pro house sends nothing and files no feed row. That silences every reminder cron and every mutation push in both domains at once.

App pieces

  • PurchasesClient (@DependencyClient): configure, offerings, purchase, restore, customer-info stream. RevenueCat's appUserID is the Convex user id, set at sign-in and logged out at sign-out.
  • ProClient: subscribes to pro:status for the current home and calls pro:refresh. The server's answer is the only one the app trusts.
  • ProFeature enum, the registry: calendar, finance, shoppingCategories, customMovieLists, archiveNow, eventFromElsewhere, expenseFromElsewhere. Every gate asks it; changing what is Pro is one line.
  • PaywallFeature takes the ProFeature that was reached for, so the sheet opens on what the person wanted.
  • ProGate view modifiers: .proLocked(_:) for a control (lock glyph, tap opens the paywall), ProLockedScreen for a whole module, ProReadOnlyBanner for lapse.

Old builds on the same backend

Every build in TestFlight talks to the one deployment, and none of them has a paywall. A refused write would show them "Something went wrong on our end". So enforcement is a switch, not a deploy:

  1. Deploy the backend with every gate written and PRO_ENFORCE unset. Behaviour is unchanged for everyone.
  2. Ship 1.12.0 to TestFlight, then the App Store. The new client gates itself from pro:status regardless of the switch.
  3. Once inbox:versionCensus shows the TestFlight households on 1.12.0, set PRO_ENFORCE=on.
  4. Existing TestFlight households get no grant. What they already put in Money and Calendar stays readable; adding more needs Pro, exactly like a lapsed subscription.

Reads never throw. A gated query for a free house returns its data, because read-only on lapse needs it, and old Today and dock screens would otherwise show alerts.

The paywall

An ordinary NestZone screen that happens to sell something, built the way the Nestie shop was settled: glass cards on the Backdrop, the theme's accent, SF Symbols, no countdowns, exactly one "Best value". It is a custom SwiftUI sheet on top of the RevenueCat SDK, not RevenueCat's hosted paywall, so it looks like the app.

  • Opens on what was tapped: "Money is part of Pro" with a still of the Money screen, then the four things Pro adds.
  • Two plans: yearly (Best value, with a 1-week free trial) and monthly. Prices come from StoreKit through RevenueCat, localised; the app never prints a hardcoded price.
  • Says "One subscription covers everyone in {house}".
  • Restore purchases, Terms of Use (Apple's standard EULA) and Privacy Policy links on the sheet itself (Guideline 3.1.2).
  • Settings gets a "NestZone Plus" row: status, who carries it, renewal date, "Manage subscription" (manageSubscriptionsSheet), Restore.
  • Success is a stamp and a haptic, never confetti, since this is money moving.
  • Copy written in both languages the way people talk: "Ev için Plus", "Bütün ev kullanır".

  • com.walhallaa.NestZone.plus.monthly, 1 month, no trial.

  • Consumables com.walhallaa.NestZone.sparkles.300, .1000, .2200: names, descriptions in English and Turkish, prices from NestZone.storekit, a review screenshot of the purchase card each.
  • In-App Purchase key (.p8) and App Store Connect API key, for RevenueCat.
  • App Store Server Notifications V2 URL: RevenueCat's, with RevenueCat forwarding to /appstore/notifications so Sparkle refunds reach the jar. That route answers 404 until the tunnel sends /appstore/.* to :3211; until then a refund is not debited (a known, small risk).
  • Two purchase paths in one app. Sparkles keep their StoreKit 2 flow, finished only after the server credits. So RevenueCat runs with purchasesAreCompletedBy: .myApp: the app finishes Plus transactions itself once pro:refresh answers, and each listener ignores the other's product ids.
  • Version: rename "1.0" to 1.12.0 to match the build; description, keywords, promo text, support and privacy URLs, screenshots (6.9" and 6.5"), age rating, App Privacy, review notes, the build.
